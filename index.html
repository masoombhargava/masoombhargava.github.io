Research Interests

What drives my work

My research focuses on mathematical and data-informed modeling to investigate complex dynamics in biological systems. I develop models grounded in nonlinear dynamics and use bifurcation theory to analyze tipping points and critical transitions in nonlinear, stochastic, and multi-scale living systems.

I am particularly interested in integrating experimental and observational data to inform model structure and parameterization. My work spans spatiotemporal dynamics in biological and ecological systems, infectious disease spread, and tumor growth, with emphasis on how key parameters drive the emergence of complex biological patterns.

Nonlinear Dynamics

Analysis of phase-space trajectories, bifurcation theory, limit cycles, and critical transitions in chaotic dynamical systems.

Mathematical Biology

Formulating mechanistic ODE/PDE models to evaluate tumor-immune interactions, chemo-immunotherapy efficacy, and epidemiological dynamics.

Periodic & Stochastic Systems

Modeling environmental stochasticity via Itô and Stratonovich SDEs, analyzing random fluctuations, and assessing the impact of seasonal periodic forcing.

Spatiotemporal Patterns

Investigating Turing instabilities and reaction-diffusion systems that drive the formation of macroscopic biological phenomena, such as animal coat patterns.

Network Science

Analyzing complex topologies to model human connectivity, sociological interactions, and the dynamic spread of news and information across social networks.

ML & DL Techniques

Applying Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) and data-driven parameter inference to complement deterministic mathematical modeling.
